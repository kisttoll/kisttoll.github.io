The Dangers of Tomato Diseases

Tomato plants, especially when grown in organic systems, can be susceptiable to various diseases that can destroy the crop. Examples are included below:

Common Tomato Fungal Diseases

Necrotic rings on tomato leaves

Early Blight

Water soaked chlorotic spots that progress to necrotic lesions

Late Blight

Brown lesions on tomato leaves with visible black pycnidia

Septoria Leaf Spot

Patches of white to olive green mold on the backside of a tomato leaf

Leaf Mold

Patches of grey/brown fungal structures

Gray Mold

Patches of white fungal growth on the top of tomato leaves

Powdery Mildew

Effects of Disease

Tomato diseases destroy 20-30% of tomato crop yield in the United States every year. Above are some of the worst fungal pathogens that tomato farmers face. Be sure to look out for the symptoms depicted on this webpage so you can avoid adding to the destoryed crop statistic.
